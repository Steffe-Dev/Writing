Chichi (part of a discriminated-against race of humanoid rodents), walks to work. He is an intuitive master of LOTU, but the reader does not know that, and nor does he. The world around him gradually starts falling apart, revealing a utopia for rodents, but a form of hell for human beings. He vividly feels this, and watches this. But, when he gets to work, everything is back to normal again, and he gets yelled at by his human boss for being 2 minutes late (have something out of his control happen, that is human's fault). The punchline is that he used LOTU to alter his consciousness, a form of drug-like escapism, so what he saw was real, to him, but no one else will ever know about it. Hint at his deep hate, and how he intends to realise this future one day, battling with the internal conflict of having a strong moral compass, and not actually wanting to cause more suffering in the world. But not seeing another way.

Shit. Again. For some, the morning begins with the crow of a rooster, or the kiss of a loved one. Maybe even the sound of children laughing. Some have one of those fancy mechanical clocks to guide them back to consciousness. As I contemplate these options, most of them rather pleasant, I am forced to question why, for me, every morning has to start with the petrifying aroma of shit. Not mine, I feel inclined to clarify. It belongs to no-one in particular, and yet it belongs to all of us here in the slums. Some, like myself, are fortunate enough to leave this place on most days, but there are those who know of no world outside of the red walls towering around us. They consist of two main groups: those that could not find employment among the humans, and children.

[Small part about his house and morning routine]
Based on the pale light that crept across my sheets, it seemed to be about an hour before sunrise. I had more than enough time, but I needed to get going. My house wasn't much of a house. It was a closet, really. It consisted of a shelf, with the top part being my bed, with a window next to it, and the bottom part having a small stove and a hole for my contribution to Our Shit. I was able to make a small fire in the stove before bed the previous night, which heated a pot filled with water and a mostly-eaten leg of mutton I found behind a tavern. If we were feeling particularly generous, we would call it 'soup'. I poured some of it into a clay pot which I carefully placed inside my bag. I removed my uniform from a rack placed near the stove. It was wet. It had rained the previous day, and I placed it there in the hope that the dim heat would be enough. It wasn't.

[Walking to the gate]
As I climbed out of the closet, and… wait. As I left my house, the first rays of sunlight started peering over the walls, illuminating a patchwork of washed-out colour that about a million people called home. Well, whether I can use the term 'people' tends on who you ask. I locked my door and started along the path towards the Gates of Heaven. I've thought about this more than I would have liked to, but I could not help revisiting the fact that the humans felt the need to call the only entryway into their world from ours the Gates of Heaven. I think it equates to formalising a god-complex, but I've never trusted anyone enough to share that sentiment. It was around this point when my detour from reality was ended a second time that morning by shit. This time clutching tightly to the sole of my hind paw, like a child to her mother. I tried to clean it, but any water I could find nearby wouldn't have led to much of an improvement.

[showing his day-pass to the humans such that he can go to work, which is what makes him late]
Claws of sunlight had fully crept over the walls by the time I reached the front of the queue. Despite there being hundreds of us waiting to ascend via their Gates of Heaven, they only bother to station one or two Legionnaires there at any given time. I've often wondered, if, on one of these miserable mornings, all of us decided to rally against that pair of pompous pricks, whether we could kill them. Another one of the thoughts no other soul could share with me, you see.

"Oi, rat! You awake?" said one of them, slapping me with enough force to send a shock through my spine.

"Uh… Yes. Sir. Yes, sir."

I spat a bit of blood to the side as I fumbled through my bag for my Pass, a tiny, engraved metal sheet that gave me the right to leave for the day's work.

"Ugh! You smell even worse than the others. Like a wet dog that soiled itself. Why is your uniform wet?"

"I'm so sorry, Sir. It rained last night, I–"

"As if it only rained for you, and not for everyone else! I don't think I can send you into the city like this! Get out of the way, you are blocking the line."

"Sir, with all due respect! I beg of you, I will lose my job if I don't show up for work!"

"I'm sure your employer will be better off without having a filthy rodent around. Don't you think, Max?"

The other Legionnaire looked up from his paperwork and snorted.

"You bet, Lance! I can smell that thing from here!"

I bowed my head and bit my lip. Tears started plotting their escape, scheming against the wavering defiance in my eyes. Hate beat inside my body with relentless echoes, like a second heart made of hollowed-out stone. I needed to keep it all down, at all costs. These guys got off on seeing our knees and foreheads in the mud. Some spittle on their shoes? All the better.

"My employer is the Imperial Chariot Workshop. We are currently servicing the King's own chariot, among others, which need to be ready for the parade tomorrow."

Lance raised an eyebrow.

"Is that so?"

I nodded sharply.

"Yes, check my Pass, if you would be so kind." I held it out to him. "They run a tight ship, I don't think they can do it without me. Nothing in the world would pain me more than seeing our beloved Lord Majesty in anything less that the finest vessel ever constructed!"

Lance took the Pass, taking special care not to touch my paw in the process.

"Let's see…" He looked over to Max, raising an eyebrow. "Won't you look at that? He's actually telling the truth." He turned back to me.

"You know what? I am feeling generous today. You may go."

He dropped my Pass into a puddle of mud in front of me.

I bowed down further than I already was.

"Thank you, Sir! Thank you so very much!" I said, as a fished out the wet piece of metal with as much grace as I could muster. When I looked up, I was startled to see Lance's face close enough to mine for his breath to blow over me. It smelled of artificial mint, with the vaguest tinge of alcohol catching a lift on the current.

"Don't ever dare show up here this dishevelled. You understand?"

I nodded.

"There is a reason why your kind does not live among us, you know. Today I was reminded of that fact"

A sizable stretch of uninhabited foliage lay between the slums and the city. I can't say that I blame them. Well, I don't blame them for putting a few clicks between themselves and shit, that is. I do blame them for pretty much everything else. I couldn't find anything sensible to say to him before I finally walked through the gates. I don't think he wanted me to say anything. I wore the expression of a wise man who just passed along an essential truism to his kin. No matter, I was finally free to head towards the city. I walked as fast as I could on my hind legs. I tried to refrain from running on all fours while they were still watching. As soon as they disappeared between the trees behind me, however, my paws hit the dirt. I knew no-one else on this road would bat an eye. They were my fellow owners of shit, you see. As the distance grew between myself and the Gates, I once again allowed myself to feel the hate. This time, the tears overwhelmed my defenses.

[The progressive ending of the world as he walks to work, reaching a pinnacle as he gets there]
It was at that moment that time seemed to tire of it's clockwork march to oblivion. My tears drifted down below me as if suspended by upward currents. Rebellion stirred within the council of reality. The rainbow, often seen as a paragon of discipline, a family operating in perfect harmony, seemed to descend into a feud. Like something had been brewing under their skin. Purple was the first to fly into a rage. Rays of light flew from the horizon like needles, lavender threads trailing in their wake. Upon hitting my frozen tears they exploded into a tapestry stretching into all directions. Her brothers and sisters had since started their pursuit. The ground beneath my feet cracked into scars of azure, and the sky had become a milky white, as if the earth was a torrent of paint hurtling towards a blank canvas. Everyone around me seemed to be mesmerised by the spectacle, unable to do much beyond staring. This was hardly surprising. Outside the slums most of us did not show much emotion, let alone opinion. However, when I looked closer I saw something magical. It was like a ripple flowed through all of them, visible only in the deepest recesses of their eyes. It was like the hate pulsating through my body had escaped, and was now pulsating within each one of them. One by one they sprung into action, as if time had been given a second wind. A river of colour and anger started flowing through the forest, heading straight for the oblivious city still slumbering on the horizon.

Up until this point, everything had been mostly silent, apart from the nature's odd tick, click, and rustle. But it was then that my comrades found their voices. It started as a simple chorus of dissent, gradually growing as the rainbow household continued to quarrel about who fucked whose wife. Shouts rung out. Most of them, I must admit, were about smelling, living in, being covered in, and generally being associated with, shit. Some of them, though, were about a world each and every one of us imagined, but did not dare speak about. As we charged towards the pristine walls surrounding the city, I could not help realising that no-one was leading us. No-one had to. We knew what we wanted. Or, I should rather say, we knew exactly what it was we didn't want.

The humans frequently joke about how the inhabitants of the slums multiply with wanton abandon, based off our likeness to lesser beings often kept as pets, but in this case it seemed an apt metaphor for what I saw. Every passing moment seemed to produce another wave of us. Were there really this many of us? Well, the humans tried to suppress any form of culture among us, for fear of us developing a sense of unity, so there had never been a reason for this many of us to gather at a single location. It was truly a spectacle, thousands of voices, teeth, and claws being enveloped by a torrent of colour.

At first the humans didn't seem to notice, but, as we drew near, horns sounded and faces appeared atop the walls.
