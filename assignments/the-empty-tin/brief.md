# The assignment

en · Probe 2 of 4: causality

## What you are practicing

Causality means one thing happens because an earlier thing made it necessary. "She puts the ticket in the tin because he said the fare was the rent" is a cause. "She puts the ticket in the tin and then understands him" is a sequence plus a verdict. Stay with the sentence that forces the next one. The talk has to move the ticket or the tin.

## Situation

Hester and her brother Piet are in the kitchen. The rent tin on the table is empty. Piet's coat is on the chair, and the rail ticket is in the pocket. Hester has to tell him what she found. Piet has to answer. Whatever he says has to make her do one thing with the ticket or the tin. Stop when that thing is done. This is one exchange. Do not write the morning, and do not write that they have forgiven each other.

## Length and form

150–400 words. Fragment.

## Constraints

Write above the include line in index.md. One scene. No notes to yourself.

## Checks

1. Pass if the first sentence gives a fact that a later action uses. Fail if it only thinks, feels, or announces a motive.
2. Pass if one spoken sentence causes the next physical move. Fail if the narrator explains that move with a feeling or a verdict.
3. Pass if that move uses a fact already on the page. Fail if a new wish arrives and does the work.
4. Pass if the last change in who holds an object, or who is in the room, follows from the speech just before it. Fail if the last sentence judges the relationship or names a motive.
5. Pass if the prose above the include line is between 150 and 400 words. Fail if it falls outside that band.

## Evidence

> It was at that moment that time seemed to tire of it's clockwork march to oblivion. My tears drifted down below me as if suspended by upward currents.

The world changes in those two sentences, and the cause on the page is that time grew tired.
