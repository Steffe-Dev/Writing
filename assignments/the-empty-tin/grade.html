The toss is the one honest cause. "tossed it into the tin" earns its place: the coat is already on the chair, the pocket is already full, and the rent tin is already empty, so the hand has a fact to obey. "Hester resembled a kettle, slowly bubbling towards a boiling point" earns nothing. Cut it. That sentence leaves the talk to announce that she is boiling, and the pocket question after it is the line that puts the tin and the pocket on the table. Her line about the snow is what shoves him. Let that line stand, and let the ticket hit the tin, with the kettle gone.
